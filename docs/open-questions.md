# Open questions

Things that are genuinely undecided. They are written down so that they get
decided by the organisation rather than settled by default, in an
implementation detail, by whoever happened to write that part first.

Each one should eventually turn into a decision with a date and a reason, not
simply disappear.

## Legal form and jurisdiction

Which country, and which form of non-profit within it. This determines far more
than paperwork: what "democratically organised" is allowed to mean, who is
personally liable, what has to be published, and how hard it is for a future
board to change the rules.

## Membership

Who is a member, what it costs if anything, and what a member actually gets to
vote on. A non-profit where members vote only on the board is a different
organisation from one where they vote on features, moderation policy, or the
budget.

## Where automation stops

Principle 4 wants executable rules. It does not say where the line falls
between a rule that runs by itself and a judgement that stays with people.
Getting this wrong in either direction is bad: an unenforced rule is a
decoration, and an automated judgement is a bureaucracy nobody can appeal to.

## Federation

Whether wellbe.social federates, and with what. Principle 3 says
decentralisation is a means and not the point, which settles the framing but
not the decision. The honest version of this question includes what federation
costs in moderation, in safety, and in the promise that nobody is profiling
you — none of which we control on somebody else's server.

## Money

How this is paid for. Memberships, donations, public funding, paid features and
advertising are all still on the table.

Principle 1 does not rule advertising out. What it rules out is the kind that
needs people profiled in order to be targeted, or a product built to hold their
attention so the advert can be shown again — which is, inconveniently, the kind
that pays best. Untargeted advertising against a product that is trying to let
you leave is a much smaller business, and whether it is a large enough one is a
real question rather than a rhetorical one.

Each option changes who the organisation ends up accountable to, and that, more
than the revenue, is what has to be decided deliberately.

## What the product actually is

"A place to keep up with the people in your life" is a direction, not a
product. The first real decision is which small part of that to build, and for
whom.
